## 📋 Course Overview & Pedagogical Objectives

The Sponsor Interview is the first decisive gateway in the J-1 Summer Work Travel (SWT) application process. Designated US Department of State sponsors (such as CIEE, InterExchange, Spirit, Intrax, Greenheart, CHI) conduct this screening in English to evaluate:
1. **Verbal English Proficiency:** (Spontaneous comprehension, fluency, conversational speed, workplace instructions, customer-facing aptitude).
2. **University Status & Non-Immigrant Intent:** (Active, full-time undergraduate or graduate enrollment in home country, clear intent and obligation to return to resume studies).
3. **Physical Stamina & Work Ethic:** (Readiness for 32–40+ hours/week of physical tasks, standing on feet, varying shifts, weekends, seasonal weather).
4. **Cultural Exchange Motivation:** (Authentic curiosity to experience American workplace culture, participate in local community events, and share Colombian/home country traditions).
5. **Living Independently & Problem Solving:** (Roommate cooperation, budgeting, handling homesickness, adherence to J-1 regulations, 24/7 sponsor contact protocols).

## BLOCK 1: Sponsor Evaluation Rubric & Impression Strategy (20 Mins)

### 1.1 The Official Sponsor Grading Standard
Sponsor interviewers evaluate applicants on a standardized English rating scale. To be approved for customer-facing positions (cashier, server assistant, front desk, lifeguard, ride operator), students must target **High Intermediate (B2)** or above.

```
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| SPONSOR RATING SCALE (J-1 SWT Assessment Standard)                                               |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| [ ] Beginner          | Elementary expressions only; heavy translation needed; slow speech.       |
| [ ] Basic (A2)        | Simple day-to-day conversation; frequent grammar breakdowns; pauses.      |
| [ ] Intermediate (B1) | Understands questions; responds beyond basics; back-of-house suitable.    |
| [*] High Intermediate | Fluent interaction; spontaneous; clear customer-service vocabulary (B2).  |
| [*] Advanced (C1)     | Quick, natural responses; robust vocabulary; flexible expression; leader. |
| [ ] Fluent            | Native-like ease; coherent reconstruction of complex arguments.           |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 1.2 The "Personality Trait Five" Matrix for Work & Travel
Goal Trait Combination for Success: `Hardworking & Resilient` + `Adaptable / Flexible` + `Team Player` + `Enthusiastic & Outgoing` + `Responsible & Independent`

## BLOCK 2: Phonetics & Conversational Mechanics for Spanish Speakers (20 Mins)

### 2.1 The Big 5 L1-Spanish Phonetic Repair Drills

1. **The `/ʃ/` vs `/tʃ/` Distinction (Shop vs Chop, Shift vs Cheat):**  
   - ❌ "I can take any *cheat* or clean the *shef*."  
   - ✅ "I can take any **shift** `/ʃɪft/` and support the **chef** `/ʃɛf/`."

2. **The Initial `/s/` Cluster Softening (No 'Es-'):**  
   - ❌ "I checked the *es-schedule* with my *es-supervisor*."  
   - ✅ "I checked the **schedule** `/ˈskɛdʒuːl/` with my **supervisor** `/ˈsuːpərvaɪzər/`."

3. **Regular Past-Tense `-ed` Endings (`/t/`, `/d/`, `/ɪd/`):**  
   - `/t/` after voiceless consonants: *worked* `/wɜːkt/`, *helped* `/hɛlpt/`.  
   - `/d/` after voiced consonants: *cleaned* `/kliːnd/`, *served* `/sɜːvd/`.  
   - `/ɪd/` ONLY after `/t/` or `/d/`: *assisted* `/əˈsɪstɪd/`, *adjusted* `/əˈdʒʌstɪd/`.

4. **The `/v/` vs `/b/` Sound Repair:**  
   - ❌ "I am *bery* *abailable* for extra *serbice*."  
   - ✅ "I am **very** `/ˈvɛri/` **available** `/əˈveɪləbəl/` for customer **service** `/ˈsɜːvɪs/`."

5. **Short `/ɪ/` vs Long `/iː/` (Shift vs Sheet, Slip vs Sleep):**  
   - ❌ "I put new *sheeps* on the bed and didn't *slip*."  
   - ✅ "I put clean **sheets** `/ʃiːts/` on the bed during my morning **shift** `/ʃɪft/`."

## BLOCK 3: Comprehensive Breakdown of the 15 Sponsor Questions (35 Mins)

1. **Why do you want to participate in the Summer Work and Travel USA program?**  
   - *Intent:* Validate authentic cultural exchange and personal growth over purely financial motives.  
   - *Formula:* Cultural curiosity + Real-world workplace experience + English immersion.

2. **What university do you attend, what is your major, and which semester are you in?**  
   - *Intent:* Confirm active, full-time student status as required by J-1 federal regulations.  
   - *Formula:* University name + Exact degree + Current semester + Expected graduation year.

3. **Have you ever worked before in customer service, hospitality, or physical labor?**  
   - *Intent:* Measure reliability, teamwork, and past workplace responsibility.  
   - *Formula:* Past role/project + Daily tasks handled + Customer satisfaction result.

4. **Many Work & Travel jobs require standing for 8 hours or doing physical tasks. How will you handle this workload?**  
   - *Intent:* Test realistic expectations of entry-level resort/hotel/restaurant stamina.  
   - *Formula:* Proof of physical energy/sports + Past routine handling long hours + Positive mindset.

5. **What kind of positions are you most interested in and why?**  
   - *Intent:* Gauge alignment with available employer placements and job flexibility.  
   - *Formula:* First preference + Willingness to excel in any assigned seasonal role + Customer focus.

6. **What will you do if your employer schedules you for night shifts, weekend shifts, or varying hours?**  
   - *Intent:* Ensure candidate will not complain or demand specific days off during peak season.  
   - *Formula:* Total schedule flexibility + Recognition of peak holiday/weekend seasons + Team commitment.

7. **How would you handle an upset or impatient American customer complaining about their order or service?**  
   - *Intent:* Evaluate customer service de-escalation, politeness, and calm under pressure.  
   - *Formula:* Listen actively without interrupting + Apologize with empathy + Offer swift solution or fetch supervisor.

8. **Have you ever lived away from your parents or shared a room with roommates before? How will you adapt to sponsor/employer housing?**  
   - *Intent:* Assess domestic independence, cleanliness, and maturity in shared staff housing.  
   - *Formula:* Past shared living experience + Household chore discipline + Respect for roommates' sleep.

9. **What would you do if you have a conflict with a roommate or coworker who doesn't clean up?**  
   - *Intent:* Check maturity and proactive, respectful interpersonal communication.  
   - *Formula:* Calm private conversation + Creating chore schedule + Escalating only if unresolved.

10. **What are your main cultural goals during your time in the United States?**  
    - *Intent:* Confirm true J-1 cultural exchange spirit (visiting landmarks, community engagement).  
    - *Formula:* Specific travel destinations + American cultural traditions to experience + Friendships.

11. **How will you share your home country culture with Americans and international coworkers?**  
    - *Intent:* Evaluate ambassadorship qualities and cultural reciprocity.  
    - *Formula:* Sharing traditional cuisine/coffee + Music and dancing + Dispelling stereotypes warmly.

12. **What would you do in an emergency, or if you feel sick and cannot make it to your scheduled shift?**  
    - *Intent:* Verify understanding of workplace protocols and sponsor emergency hotlines.  
    - *Formula:* Call supervisor 2+ hours ahead + Visit urgent care/clinic + Contact sponsor emergency support if severe.

13. **Are you allowed to start a second job without sponsor approval? What are the rules regarding second jobs?**  
    - *Intent:* CRITICAL J-1 VISA COMPLIANCE TEST. Unauthorized work leads to SEVIS termination and deportation.  
    - *Formula:* Firm rejection of unauthorized work + Priority on primary job + Mandatory sponsor vetting and approval.

14. **How will this Summer Work and Travel experience help you with your academic and future professional career?**  
    - *Intent:* Establish long-term value that will be applied back home in Colombia.  
    - *Formula:* Professional English fluency + Global teamwork competencies + Competitive advantage in home market.

15. **What are your specific plans after your J-1 program ends, and when does your next university semester start?**  
    - *Intent:* MANDATORY NON-IMMIGRANT INTENT & HOME TIES VERIFICATION under US federal immigration law.  
    - *Formula:* Exact return date before grace period ends + Resume university classes + Graduate degree in home country.

## BLOCK 4: Extensive Interview Dialogues & Scenario Pronunciation Drills (25 Mins)

### SCENARIO 1: University Status & Summer Work Travel Motivation
```
INTERVIEWER : "Hello! Could you tell me about your university studies in Colombia, and why you decided to apply for the Summer Work and Travel program this season?" ↘
PARTICIPANT : "Hello! It is a pleasure to meet you. ↘ I am currently in my sixth semester of Industrial Engineering at Universidad Javeriana in Bogotá. ↘ I decided to apply for the Summer Work and Travel program because I want to challenge myself in a fast-paced American workplace, achieve complete English fluency, and experience American cultural diversity firsthand during my university summer break! ↘"
```

### SCENARIO 2: Physical Stamina & High-Volume Peak Season Shifts
```
INTERVIEWER : "During peak summer weeks, amusement parks and resorts operate at maximum capacity. You might be on your feet for eight hours in hot weather or busy kitchens. Have you ever handled heavy physical workloads before?" ↗
PARTICIPANT : "Yes, absolutely! ↘ At university, I play on the varsity soccer team and train four days a week, so I have great physical endurance. ↘ In addition, I helped manage logistics during our university cultural festivals, standing on my feet for nine-hour shifts coordinating food stands and guest lines. ↘ I stay hydrated, keep a positive attitude, and thrive in energetic team environments! ↘"
```

### SCENARIO 3: Customer Service Recovery & De-escalation
```
INTERVIEWER : "Imagine a customer approaches your counter visibly upset, claiming their order took too long and their burger is cold. How would you respond to them?" ↗
PARTICIPANT : "First, I listen attentively with an open posture and do not interrupt them. ↘ Then, I apologize sincerely with empathy: ↘ 'I am so sorry for the delay and for the inconvenience, let me fix this for you right away.' ↘ Next, I immediately replace the food with a fresh hot order or call my shift lead if a refund is needed. ↘ My goal is to make sure the guest leaves with a smile and feels genuinely valued! ↘"
```

### SCENARIO 4: Multicultural Staff Housing & Roommate Living
```
INTERVIEWER : "In employer housing, you will share a bedroom and kitchen with participants from countries like Jamaica, Turkey, and Thailand. How will you manage cleanliness and cultural differences?" ↗
PARTICIPANT : "I am truly excited about living with people from different countries! ↘ Living harmoniously starts with clear communication and mutual respect. ↘ On our first week, I will propose a simple rotating cleaning schedule for the kitchen and bathroom. ↘ If any misunderstanding happens, I will speak privately and kindly with my roommate. ↘ Sharing home-cooked meals together is a wonderful way to build genuine friendships! ↘"
```

### SCENARIO 5: J-1 Visa Regulations, Second Jobs & Return Intent
```
INTERVIEWER : "A coworker tells you about an unregistered cash job washing dishes at a local diner. Can you start working there immediately? And what are your plans after September?" ↘
PARTICIPANT : "No, absolutely not! ↘ J-1 federal regulations strictly forbid working any second job without prior sponsor vetting and written authorization. ↘ Doing so would terminate my SEVIS status immediately. ↘ My priority is excelling at my primary host employer. ↘ When my contract ends in September, I will use my travel grace period to visit national parks, and then fly directly home to Colombia to begin my seventh university semester. ↘ My degree and my future are rooted in Colombia! ↘"
```

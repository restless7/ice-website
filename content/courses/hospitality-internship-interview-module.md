## 📋 Course Overview & Pedagogical Objectives

The Sponsor and Host Employer Interview is the primary screening gate for the J-1 Hospitality Internship Program. Unlike general seasonal Work & Travel positions, luxury hotels (Marriott, Hyatt, Hilton, Ritz-Carlton, Four Seasons, Kalahari) and designated J-1 sponsors (CetUSA, Alliance Abroad Group, Cultural Vistas, ITN) require candidates to demonstrate executive polish, operational competence, and native-level communicative resilience.

### Core Objectives:
1. **Verbal English Proficiency:** High B2 to C1 fluency, spontaneous elaboration without translation pauses, and mastery of professional hospitality terminology.
2. **DS-7002 Placement Plan Fluency:** Explaining departmental rotations, measurable learning objectives, mentor oversight, and distinguishing educational training from routine manual labor.
3. **5-Star Guest Service & Recovery:** Demonstrating emotional intelligence, active listening, and conflict resolution using the **L.A.S.T.** (*Listen, Apologize, Solve, Thank*) and **H.E.A.T.** frameworks.
4. **Phonetic Polishing for Spanish Speakers:** Eliminating silent-letter mispronunciations in gastronomic terms (*receipt, salmon, subtle, suite, vegetable*) and refining connected speech.
5. **J-1 Non-Immigrant Intent (Section 214(b) INA):** Articulating explicit ties to Colombia’s luxury tourism and hotel development market upon program conclusion.

## BLOCK 1: Host Employer Rubric & Executive Presence (20 Mins)

### 1.1 The Sponsor & Host Hotel Grading Scale
To pass interviews with international hotel chains and J-1 sponsors, applicants must score at **High Intermediate (B2)** or **Advanced (C1)**.

```
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| J-1 HOSPITALITY INTERNSHIP EVALUATION MATRIX (Form DS-7002 Competency Standard)                  |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| [ ] Basic (A2)        | Relies on fragments; cannot explain operational tasks; heavy translation. |
| [ ] Intermediate (B1) | Understands questions; relies on repetitive vocabulary; pauses on conflict.|
| [*] High Inter. (B2)  | Smooth conversational flow; explains hotel processes; resolves guest issues.|
| [*] Advanced (C1)     | Nuanced tone; executive polish; anticipates guest needs; Forbes-level grace. |
| [ ] Native / Fluent   | Idiomatic mastery; effortless diplomacy under intense operational pressure.|
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 1.2 The "Five Star Hospitality" Trait Matrix
Host employers evaluate candidates against five critical behavioral competencies:
1. **Polished & Professional:** Impeccable posture, eye contact, grooming awareness, and diplomatic language.
2. **Guest-Obsessed (Empathetic):** Genuine desire to exceed guest expectations and turn complaints into loyalty.
3. **Agile & Adaptable:** Thriving during high-volume breakfast rushes, banquet transitions, or sudden PMS system outages.
4. **Crisis-Resilient:** Staying calm, steady, and composed when handling difficult guests or medical emergencies.
5. **Detail-Oriented:** Precision in allergen safety, table settings, VIP notes, and hygiene regulations (HACCP).

## BLOCK 2: Hospitality & Gastronomy Phonetics & Industry Idioms (25 Mins)

### 2.1 The Top 5 Hospitality Phonetic Traps for Spanish Speakers

1. **Silent Letters in Gastronomy & Billing:**
   - ❌ "Here is your *re-ceipt* with the *sal-mon* and *sub-tle* sauce."
   - ✅ "Here is your **receipt** `/rɪˈsiːt/` with the **salmon** `/ˈsæmən/` and **subtle** `/ˈsʌtl/` sauce."
   - *Rule:* The 'p' in receipt, 'l' in salmon, and 'b' in subtle are completely silent.

2. **Vowel Reduction & Syllable Compression:**
   - ❌ "The room is *com-for-ta-ble* and we serve fresh *ve-ge-ta-bles*."
   - ✅ "The room is **comfortable** `/ˈkʌmftəbl/` (3 syllables) and we serve fresh **vegetables** `/ˈvɛdʒtəblz/` (3 syllables)."
   - *Rule:* Compress unstressed middle vowels in fast hotel English.

3. **Homophones & False Friends:**
   - ❌ "I will show you to your executive *suit* /suːt/ and bring your *desert* /ˈdɛzərt/."
   - ✅ "I will escort you to your executive **suite** `/swiːt/` (sounds like 'sweet') and bring your **dessert** `/dɪˈzɜːrt/` (stress on 2nd syllable)."

4. **Polite Modal Formula Connected Speech:**
   - ❌ "Do - you - want - more - water?" (Too abrupt for luxury service).
   - ✅ "**Would ⁀ you ⁀ like** `/wʊdʒuː laɪk/` another bottle of sparkling water, Mr. Henderson?"
   - ✅ "**May ⁀ I ⁀ assist ⁀ you** with your luggage?"

5. **The Critical `/v/` vs `/b/` in Operations:**
   - ❌ "Let me check the *beserbation* for our *bip* guest."
   - ✅ "Let me verify the **reservation** `/ˌrɛzərˈveɪʃən/` for our **VIP** `/ˌviː aɪ ˈpiː/` guest."

### 2.2 Top 6 Hospitality Phrasal Verbs & Industry Idioms

1. **"Walk a guest":** Relocating a confirmed reservation to a comparable luxury sister property due to unexpected hotel overbooking, covering all transport and dinner costs.
2. **"Comp an item / service":** Providing a dish, amenity, or room night complimentary as a gesture of goodwill during service recovery.
3. **"Run the pass / Expediting":** Coordinating the critical staging area where finished culinary plates are inspected for quality before delivery to the dining room.
4. **"Turn-down service":** Evening housekeeping protocol where bed linens are folded down, ambient lighting is set, soft music is enabled, and bedside amenities are placed.
5. **"Attend to / Look into":** Executive hospitality phrasing to replace basic phrases like "I will check" or "Wait a minute".
6. **"Table turn / In the weeds":** Industry terms describing table turnover speed and feeling overwhelmed during peak service rushes.

## BLOCK 3: 20 Core Sponsor, Hotel & Consular Questions (40 Mins)

### Category A: Background & Education
* **Q1:** Tell me about your academic background in hospitality and why you chose this industry.
* **Q2:** Why did you choose our specific hotel brand and location for your J-1 internship?
* **Q3:** What prior practical experience do you have in food and beverage or front office operations?
* **Q4:** What are your greatest professional strengths, and how do you handle constructive criticism from managers?

### Category B: Food & Beverage and Front Desk Operations
* **Q5:** How do you handle a guest with a severe food allergy or strict dietary restriction?
* **Q6:** What Property Management Systems (PMS) or Point-of-Sale (POS) software have you used?
* **Q7:** How do you maintain high energy and accuracy during a 200-cover dinner rush or busy banquet?
* **Q8:** How do you handle suggestive selling and upselling without appearing aggressive or intrusive?

### Category C: Conflict Resolution & The L.A.S.T. Method
* **Q9:** Tell me about a time a guest was furious about their meal or room. How did you resolve it?
* **Q10:** How do you handle unexpected schedule changes, working overtime, or covering an understaffed shift?
* **Q11:** How do you collaborate with colleagues from completely different cultural and linguistic backgrounds?
* **Q12:** How would you handle an intoxicated or disruptive guest in the lobby lounge while preserving decorum?

### Category D: The DS-7002 Training Plan & Learning Goals
* **Q13:** What specific rotational phases and learning objectives are detailed in your Form DS-7002?
* **Q14:** How does this J-1 internship differ from ordinary employment in the United States?
* **Q15:** Who will be your primary supervisor or mentor, and how will your progress be evaluated?
* **Q16:** What would you do if your host property asked you to perform tasks outside your approved DS-7002?

### Category E: J-1 Visa Non-Immigrant Intent & Ties to Colombia
* **Q17:** Why do you need to complete this internship in the United States rather than remaining in Colombia?
* **Q18:** What are your specific professional plans immediately following the completion of your 12-month program?
* **Q19:** Why will luxury hotel properties in Colombia value your J-1 US internship experience so highly?
* **Q20:** What strong academic, personal, or financial ties guarantee your return to Colombia?

## BLOCK 4: 10 Real-World Turn-by-Turn Scenarios & Dialogues (35 Mins)

### Scenario 1: Front Desk Service Recovery — Double-Booked Suite (L.A.S.T.)
* **Context:** An executive VIP guest arrives after a 10-hour flight for their wedding anniversary, only to find their reserved Oceanfront Suite has an emergency plumbing issue.
* **Technique:** Active listening, sincere apology, immediate upgrade to Presidential Penthouse Suite, complimentary champagne and dining credit.

### Scenario 2: Food & Beverage — Severe Food Allergy Protocol (HACCP)
* **Context:** A dinner guest reveals severe celiac disease and peanut anaphylaxis during a packed Friday dinner rush.
* **Technique:** Allergen alert entered in POS, direct briefing of Executive Chef, dedicated sanitized prep station, personal hand-delivery.

### Scenario 3: J-1 Sponsor Interview — Explaining Form DS-7002
* **Context:** J-1 sponsor interviewer evaluates whether the candidate understands the educational structure of their TIPP.
* **Technique:** Systematically breaks down Phase 1 (foundational SOPs), Phase 2 (rotational operations), and Phase 3 (managerial shadowing and analytics).

### Scenario 4: High-Volume Banquet Coordination — Entrée Delay
* **Context:** An entrée delay of 15 minutes occurs during a 350-guest medical gala due to an oven breakdown.
* **Technique:** Coordination with banquet captain, proactive table replenishment of bread and beverages, synchronized room service delivery.

### Scenario 5: Consular Officer Screening — J-1 Visa Non-Immigrant Intent
* **Context:** Consular officer in Bogotá probes why the candidate will not stay in the US permanently.
* **Technique:** Solid non-immigrant intent articulation, concrete return date, Colombian tourism expansion, and university thesis defense.

### Scenario 6: Concierge & VIP Guest Experience — Curating an Itinerary
* **Context:** A high-net-worth couple staying for 48 hours asks the Concierge for an unforgettable private dining and cultural itinerary.
* **Technique:** Anticipatory questioning, booking exclusive Michelin-starred private tables, arranging private museum docent tours.

### Scenario 7: In-Room Dining & Upselling — Late-Night Executive Arrival
* **Context:** An exhausted executive arrives at 11:30 PM needing a wholesome dinner and wine pairing before an 8:00 AM board meeting.
* **Technique:** Gracious telephone etiquette, recommending light high-protein options, offering sommelier half-bottle pairings, setting wake-up breakfast carts.

### Scenario 8: Housekeeping Quality Escalation — Rapid Cleanliness Recovery
* **Context:** A guest checks into their Club Level King room and discovers a hair on the bathroom vanity and missing bathrobes.
* **Technique:** Immediate sincere ownership, dispatching the Floor Housekeeping Supervisor within 5 minutes, offering an evening cocktail lounge pass.

### Scenario 9: Sommelier & Fine Dining Wine Pairing
* **Context:** A party of four ordering dry-aged ribeye steaks and pan-seared sea bass seeks guidance on pairing across disparate flavor profiles.
* **Technique:** Descriptive tasting notes, bridging palate preferences with a bold Oregon Pinot Noir or a versatile Napa Cabernet blend.

### Scenario 10: Security & Lost Property Protocol
* **Context:** A guest leaves a designer briefcase containing passports and jewelry in the grand ballroom after a midnight gala.
* **Technique:** Escorting guest to security, strict chain-of-custody logging in the safe deposit registry, verifying identification before handoff.

## BLOCK 5: Simulation Drills, STAR Case Studies & Handout (20 Mins)

### The 4 Real-World STAR Case Studies:
1. **Case Study 1 (F&B / Allergen Crisis):**
   * *Situation:* Busy dinner service at a 4-star boutique hotel.
   * *Task:* Prevent cross-contamination for a guest with severe shellfish anaphylaxis.
   * *Action:* Flagged order in Micros POS, personally walked line to sanitize chef station, hand-delivered meal.
   * *Result:* 100% safe dining, $50 gratuity, 5-star TripAdvisor review mentioning intern by name.

2. **Case Study 2 (Front Desk / Overbooking):**
   * *Situation:* Convention overbooking with zero standard rooms available.
   * *Task:* Accommodate an exhausted traveling family.
   * *Action:* Upgraded family to the Hospitality Executive Suite, arranged complimentary breakfast vouchers.
   * *Result:* Converted an upset family into loyal brand rewards members.

3. **Case Study 3 (Operations / POS System Crash):**
   * *Situation:* Sudden network crash disabling all POS terminals during Sunday Brunch.
   * *Task:* Maintain order flow and guest payment processing for 180 guests.
   * *Action:* Implemented manual guest check protocol, coordinated runners with kitchen whiteboard, calculated manual bills accurately.
   * *Result:* Maintained table turn speed without a single lost charge or billing error.

4. **Case Study 4 (Banquets / VIP Wedding Protocol):**
   * *Situation:* Heavy rainstorm threatening an outdoor garden wedding reception.
   * *Task:* Relocate 200 guests and floral staging into the Grand Ballroom in under 20 minutes.
   * *Action:* Orchestrated banquet and housekeeping teams to flip the ballroom, reset lighting, and serve welcome cocktails.
   * *Result:* Bride and groom ecstatic; banquet captain commended intern's leadership.
